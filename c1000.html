ENGL C1000E

Embedded Support Resources

ENGL C1000E includes the same academic reading and writing core as C1000, with embedded support determined locally. I list these resources separately because they are especially useful for targeted scaffolding, guided practice, multilingual writing support, language development, and editing. I do not score them as complete C1000 textbooks.

Academic Reading & Sources

Integrated Skills: Academic Writing with Sources

Trischa Duke, Becky Bonarek, and Steph Mielcarek · University of Illinois Chicago / LibreTexts · CC BY-NC-SA 4.0

Best role: Highly scaffolded academic reading, source-use, and writing-process support

Explicit practice in active reading, annotation, paraphrase, summary, response, source integration, argumentative research, peer feedback, revision, and portfolio work.

Academic reading Multilingual writers Source integration Writing process Research scaffolding Peer review
Watch for: Designed for a pre-composition English for Academic Purposes course. Excellent for targeted support, but narrower than a full C1000 curriculum.

Critical Reading & Argument

A Guide to Analyzing Arguments in an Academic Setting

Joshua Botvin, Yuan Zhang, and Jacqueline E. O'Dell · UMass Dartmouth · CC BY-NC-SA

Best role: Highly scaffolded argument-reading and analysis support

Concise, student-friendly instruction in thesis, claims, evidence, counterargument, implicit claims, audience, purpose, tone, agenda, bias, skeptical reading, and annotation.

Critical reading Argument analysis Claims & evidence Counterargument Annotation
Watch for: A focused supplement rather than a broad writing curriculum. It does not provide full genre, research, documentation, or formal logic instruction.

Multilingual Academic Writing

University Academic Writing for International Students: A Usage-based Approach

Carol Lynn Moder et al. · Oklahoma State University Libraries · CC BY 4.0 except where otherwise noted

Best role: Genre-based academic writing, source-use, and language support for multilingual students

Modular support for audience and purpose, writing from readings and data, summary, persuasive writing, source evaluation and integration, revision, and grammar taught in authentic genre contexts.

Multilingual writers Academic language Genre Source evaluation Summary & paraphrase Revision
Watch for: Designed specifically for international students rather than as a complete C1000 text. Some terminology reflects older native/non-native assumptions and benefits from a linguistic-justice framing.

Language, Power & Media Literacy

Critical Language Awareness: Language Power Techniques and English Grammar

Jonathon Reinhardt, Anuj Gupta, Robert Poole, and Dilara Avci · University of Arizona · CC BY-NC 4.0 except where otherwise noted

Best role: Critical language awareness, rhetoric, grammar, and media-literacy support

Connects grammar and language choices to ideology, power, metaphor, doublespeak, pronoun choice, propaganda, logical fallacies, media literacy, and corpus analysis.

Linguistic diversity Grammar in context Media literacy Bias & propaganda Corpus analysis
Watch for: Best used selectively. Some examples are politically charged by design and benefit from instructor framing and discussion of context.

Editing & Conventions

Grammar Essentials

Excelsior Online Writing Lab · CC BY 4.0

Best role: Just-in-time grammar, punctuation, and editing practice

Modular, student-friendly reference and practice covering punctuation, parts of speech, quotation integration, sentence-level conventions, and common editing issues. The live OWL includes interactive practice.

Editing Sentence-level support Punctuation Grammar & usage Interactive practice
Watch for: Best used selectively as an editing and conventions resource, not as a complete writing curriculum. Frame conventions rhetorically and distinguish academic expectations from language difference.

How I review C1000 Content Alignment

I apply the C1000 Content Alignment Rubric to the instructional content of each resource. The seven areas are Academic Reading, Rhetoric & Rhetorical Awareness, Writing Process, Academic Genres, Research & Source Use, Documentation, and Language, Style & Rhetorical Choice.

Ratings reflect explicit instructional coverage, not simply the presence of a topic, reading, assignment, or opportunity to practice. Strong Alignment means substantial, teachable instruction; Meaningful Alignment means useful explicit instruction that may need supplementation; Limited Alignment means the area is present but brief or secondary; Not a Significant Focus means little or no direct instruction.

Use View alignment details on any OER card to see the rationale, evidence reviewed, and confidence behind each criterion.

I am currently the sole evaluator for these editorial Content Alignment reviews. Faculty Experience is collected separately and never changes the Content Alignment ratings. The IDEA Framework review is also a separate lens.

These are independent editorial assessments, not ASCCC/OERI ratings or endorsements.
