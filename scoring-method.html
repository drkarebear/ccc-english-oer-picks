Transparency & Methodology

General Scoring Method

How CCC English OER Picks turns course expectations into practical, course-specific alignment observations. AI assists with a structured first pass; I check the source evidence, revise the draft ratings and notes, and make the final editorial judgment before publication.

What the ratings are for

The alignment ratings help faculty compare a large number of OER before investing time in a full adoption review. They are not rankings of overall quality and are not official determinations that a resource satisfies a Common Course Numbering course, local course outline, articulation requirement, accessibility standard, or institutional curriculum process.

Each rating answers a narrower question: How much meaningful, teachable coverage of this particular course criterion appears to be present in this resource?

Step 1: Build a course-specific rubric

I developed a separate working rubric for each course using statewide CCN expectations and related curriculum guidance. The rubric converts broad course expectations into distinct instructional domains so that very different OER can be examined using the same course-specific questions.

Start with course expectations

The rubric begins with what students are expected to read, analyze, research, reason through, and write in the course.

Separate the domains

Each major area is scored independently. A resource can be strong in one domain and limited in another without receiving one reductive overall grade.

Define visible evidence

Rubric descriptions distinguish explicit, teachable instruction from simple topic presence, a chapter title, an assignment prompt, or a passing mention.

Revise the working tool

AI assisted with drafting, organizing, and testing rubric language, but I reviewed and revised the project-developed rubric before applying it. The rubrics remain open to correction and refinement.

The four-point alignment scale

The criteria change by course, but the meaning of the dots stays consistent.

●●●Strong alignment

Substantial, explicit, teachable coverage that could support a major part of the course criterion.

●●○Meaningful alignment

Useful coverage, though faculty may want additional readings, examples, assignments, or instruction.

●○○Limited alignment

The criterion is present but mainly supplemental rather than substantial.

○○○Not a significant focus

The criterion is absent, minimal, or outside the intended purpose of the resource.

Three dots does not mean “best book.” The dots describe alignment with one criterion in one course. A focused resource can be excellent at a narrower job and still earn one or zero dots elsewhere.

Step 2: AI-assisted first pass, then human editorial review

The published review uses a two-stage process. AI helps with scale and organization; the final editorial judgment is human.

Stage 1

AI-assisted pass against the course rubric

The OER is compared with the course-specific rubric. The first pass identifies possible evidence, drafts criterion-level dot ratings, and notes likely strengths or gaps. This is a draft, not the published conclusion.

Stage 2

My human editorial pass

I open the OER and check the underlying material. I verify that the evidence actually supports the criterion, revise ratings or notes that are unsupported or overstated, and add context about likely course roles and supplementation needs before publishing.

AI is not the evidence. Generative AI can miss context, invent details, misunderstand a resource's purpose, or overlook material. A model's confidence does not count as verification. The resource itself is the evidence used for the final editorial pass.

Ratings are course-specific

The same OER may receive different observations in C1000, C1001, C1002, and C1003 because those courses ask students and instructors to do different work. The goal is not to assign one permanent score to a book. The goal is to understand the role it may play in a particular course.

Coverage, not prestige

Ratings describe instructional coverage, not reputation, popularity, production quality, or whether I personally like the resource.

Role matters

A resource may work best as a primary text, reader, anthology, or targeted supplement. Not every OER needs to do everything.

Combinations may be stronger

A strong pairing can be more useful than searching indefinitely for a single resource that covers every need.

Ratings can change

OER are revised, links change, and better evidence can emerge. Published observations are treated as revisable.

Scope of the current review

The project previously explored a separate ASCCC OERI IDEA Framework crosswalk. I am not currently publishing formal IDEA scores or badges because I cannot apply that layer consistently across every resource at the level of care it deserves. Qualitative notes may still identify relevant representation, inclusion, equity, anti-racism, or accessibility considerations.

Editorial alignment and faculty experience are different evidence

The site intentionally does not blend editorial alignment with faculty recommendations into a single score.

Editorial alignment

Asks: What does this resource appear to teach in relation to this course? It comes from the course rubric, source evidence, and editorial review process above.

Faculty experience

Asks: What happened when someone actually taught with it? Recommendations and teaching notes can reveal classroom realities that an initial review cannot.

What the other labels mean

Start Here

A resource that appears especially worth investigating early. It is an editorial shortcut, not an award or official endorsement.

Best Role

A practical judgment about whether the resource may work best as a core text, reader, anthology, or supplement.

Watch For

A note about something faculty may want to examine more closely, such as a coverage gap, mixed licensing, an older version, representation concerns, or likely supplementation needs.

What these ratings should not be used for

These observations should not be treated as official CCN compliance reviews, curriculum approvals, articulation decisions, accessibility certifications, licensing audits, or endorsements by ASCCC, ASCCC OERI, or the California Community Colleges Chancellor's Office.

Faculty remain responsible for reviewing resources in relation to their own course outline, local requirements, student needs, accessibility expectations, licensing, and instructional goals.
