Start with your problem, not with a prompt number

Faculty arrive here at very different stages. You may be searching a niche field with almost no obvious OER, trying to find one missing topic, or staring at dozens of possible textbooks. Pick the situation that sounds most like yours.

If you are brand new to OER: begin with Discover OER. If you already have several plausible resources, skip ahead to Narrow by Content.

Not sure? Follow the full path

You do not need every bank every time. This is the typical path when you are starting from scratch.

1DISCOVERSee what exists.
2SHORTLISTKeep plausible options.
3CONTENTEvaluate course fit.
4IDEAApply the separate lens.
5COMPARESurface tradeoffs.
6DECIDEFaculty choose.
Do not deeply evaluate everything you find. If the field is large, use discovery and quick screening to get to a manageable shortlist first. Detailed Content Rubric and IDEA review should be reserved for the resources you are seriously considering.

Two lenses, one faculty decision

Content Rubric

Question: How well does this OER teach the content and practices of my course?

See the scoring method →

ASCCC OERI IDEA Framework

Question: What inclusion, diversity, equity, and anti-racism strengths, gaps, and revision opportunities appear?

Keep this lens separate from Content Rubric scores.

Do not turn the two lenses into one composite score. A resource may be especially strong in course content while showing IDEA revision opportunities, or vice versa. The useful question is what those findings mean for your teaching decision.

Three rules across every prompt bank

Verify the resource and license

Free-to-read is not automatically OER. Ask for direct links and verified open-license information whenever possible.

AI is not the evidence

Important judgments should point to material a faculty member can inspect: chapters, sections, pages, assignments, examples, metadata, or stable URLs.

Uncertainty is allowed

“Insufficient evidence” is more useful than a confident guess. Sparse fields should stay sparse rather than being padded with weak candidates.

Faculty make the decision

Prompts can organize the search and comparison. They cannot decide whether an OER should be adopted for your particular course and students.

Want to understand the method first?

The prompt banks are designed for faculty who want to get to work quickly. The methodology remains available for anyone who wants to inspect how the project separates content alignment, IDEA review, evidence, and faculty judgment.
