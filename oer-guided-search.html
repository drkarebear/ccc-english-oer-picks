GUIDED OER SEARCH · NEWER TO AI

Find OER One Step at a Time

Use four simple prompts to move from your Course Outline of Record to a short list of OERs worth exploring yourself.

You do not need one perfect prompt

This guided path breaks the search into small decisions. Complete one step, look at what the AI gives you, and then move to the next step. You are not asking AI to choose your textbook. You are using it to make the search more manageable.

Before you begin: have your Course Outline of Record (COR) ready. If you already have a list of possible OERs from the ASCCC OERI, your college, a librarian, or another trusted source, keep that list handy too.

The Guided Search

Use the prompts in order. You can copy each one into ChatGPT or another AI tool.

1

Help AI understand your course

Start with the COR before searching for materials.

What to provide: Attach your COR, upload it, or paste the relevant text into the chat.
I am looking for free or zero-cost course materials for this class. Please review the Course Outline of Record (COR) I provided. Before we search for materials, briefly identify: • the major topics students need to learn • the skills students need to develop • any topics or requirements that an OER would especially need to cover Keep this concise. I will use this information to help us evaluate possible OERs.
Before moving on: Does this sound like your course? Correct anything the AI misunderstood or missed.
2

Compare known OER options

Start with resources that have already been identified as possibilities.

What to provide: Paste your list of possible OER titles and links. This could come from the ASCCC OERI, a librarian, your discipline, or another trusted source.
Here are OERs that have already been identified as possible resources for this course: [PASTE OER TITLES AND LINKS HERE] Please research these resources and compare them with my COR. Identify the 3–5 most promising options. For each, tell me: • what it covers well • what may be missing • why it might or might not work for this course Please do not assume an OER is a good fit simply because it appears on this list. If you cannot verify a resource or its contents, say so.
Before moving on: Notice which resources keep appearing as strong fits. Do not choose one yet.
3

Look for possibilities you may have missed

Now expand beyond your starting list.

What to provide: Nothing new is required. Keep working in the same AI conversation so it still has your COR and earlier results.
Now look for other reputable OERs that might be a strong fit for this course but were not included on my list. Prioritize established OER sources, including resources from colleges and universities, OpenStax, LibreTexts, Pressbooks, and other reputable OER repositories. Only recommend resources you can verify actually exist and are currently accessible. Give me a direct link to each resource when possible, identify the open license if you can verify it, and briefly explain why each resource is worth considering. If the field is sparse, tell me that instead of padding the list with weak options.
Before moving on: Open a few links. Make sure the resources actually exist, are accessible, and appear to be what the AI says they are.
4

Create a manageable shortlist

Ask AI to narrow the field, not to make the final decision.

What to provide: Stay in the same conversation so AI can consider the COR, your original list, and the additional resources it found.
Considering everything we have reviewed, which 3–5 resources should I actually open and explore myself? Rank them from strongest to weakest fit for my COR. For each one, give me: • Resource title and direct link • Why I should look at it • One potential weakness or gap • What I should examine when I open it Keep this short. I want a manageable list that I can evaluate myself rather than having AI make the final decision for me.
Before moving on: Your goal is a shortlist, not a winner. AI has helped organize the search. The next judgment is yours.
Human Checkpoint

Stop and explore the resources yourself

Open the strongest candidates. Click through the table of contents. Read part of a chapter. Look at the examples, navigation, accessibility, organization, and tone. Ask yourself whether you would actually want to teach with this resource and whether your students could realistically use it.

The COR sets the requirements. The OER supports the course. Faculty make the decision.
