ENGL C1002 Content Rubric · Content Lens

Introduction to Literature

The project-developed content rubric used to examine how well an OER supports literary breadth, genre study, close reading, critical approaches, and research/writing in ENGL C1002.

What this content rubric does

This rubric is built around the instructional work of an introductory literature course. The categories are scored independently because one resource may offer excellent literary breadth across poetry, drama, and fiction while another may be especially strong in close reading, literary terms, critical approaches, research, or writing about literature.

No single overall score. The rubric is intentionally literature-centered and does not reward or penalize resources for formal logic or argument instruction that belongs more centrally to C1003.

This is the content lens. It asks whether an OER provides meaningful, teachable coverage of C1002's literary and writing work. It is separate from the site's ASCCC OERI IDEA Framework crosswalk, which examines inclusion, diversity, equity, and anti-racism. The two lenses are never merged into one score.

The shared four-point content-alignment scale

●●●
Strong alignment
Substantial, teachable coverage that could support a major part of this criterion.
●●○
Meaningful alignment
Useful coverage; some supplementation may help.
●○○
Limited alignment
Present, but mainly supplemental.
○○○
Not a significant focus
Minimal, absent, or outside the resource's intended purpose.

How this content rubric was built

1. Start with the course.

The rubric organizes the instructional work of introductory literary study into distinct domains: breadth, major genres, diverse voices, literary terminology and approaches, close reading, and research-based writing.

2. Evaluate instruction, not presence.

A resource does not earn strong alignment merely because it contains poems, stories, plays, literary terms, or essay prompts. Stronger ratings require substantial literary material and/or meaningful instruction, modeling, guided analysis, or scaffolding.

3. Keep the evidence visible.

Reviewers support each rating with a brief rationale and a specific location—chapter, section, page, heading, or stable URL anchor whenever possible.

Working rubric: This is a faculty-developed analytical tool, not an official CCC, CCN, articulation, or curriculum determination. The rubric may be revised as evidence and faculty feedback improve the project.

The C1002 content criteria

Open any criterion to see exactly what distinguishes Strong, Meaningful, Limited, and Not a Significant Focus.

1 Literary Breadth View rating descriptions
●●● Strong alignment

Provides substantial literary study across multiple major genres and traditions, with enough range to serve as a meaningful spine for an introductory literature course rather than concentrating narrowly on one genre, author, period, or theme.

●●○ Meaningful alignment

Provides meaningful breadth across several literary forms or traditions and can support a significant portion of an introductory literature course, though some genres or areas may need supplementation.

●○○ Limited alignment

Provides useful literary material but is concentrated in a narrow genre, tradition, theme, period, or selection. Faculty would need substantial additional literature to achieve broad introductory coverage.

○○○ Not a significant focus

Provides little or no substantial literary content suitable for supporting broad introductory literary study.

Calibration example: Strong = substantial units in poetry, drama, and fiction with multiple works; Limited = one genre only with no meaningful breadth.
2 Poetry View rating descriptions
●●● Strong alignment

Provides substantial poetry selections and explicit instruction in reading and interpreting poetic form, imagery, figurative language, sound, rhythm, voice, structure, and other relevant poetic elements.

●●○ Meaningful alignment

Provides meaningful poetry selections and useful instruction in several elements of poetic analysis, though the range of poems or depth of instruction may need supplementation.

●○○ Limited alignment

Includes some poetry or introductory poetic terminology but provides limited range or limited direct instruction in close reading and interpretation of poems.

○○○ Not a significant focus

Provides little or no meaningful poetry content or instruction.

Calibration example: Strong = multiple poems paired with instruction in form, imagery, sound, and guided close reading; Limited = a few poems with little or no analytical apparatus.
3 Drama View rating descriptions
●●● Strong alignment

Provides substantial dramatic literature and explicit support for studying dramatic structure, character, dialogue, performance context, genre conventions, language, and interpretation.

●●○ Meaningful alignment

Provides meaningful dramatic texts and useful instruction in several elements of drama, though the range of plays or depth of instruction may need supplementation.

●○○ Limited alignment

Includes a play, excerpts, or dramatic terminology but offers limited dramatic breadth or direct instruction in analyzing drama.

○○○ Not a significant focus

Provides little or no meaningful dramatic literature or instruction.

Calibration example: Strong = at least one substantial dramatic work plus instruction in structure and performance context; Limited = a summary or excerpt with little analytical instruction.
4 Fiction View rating descriptions
●●● Strong alignment

Provides substantial short fiction and/or longer fictional works with explicit instruction in elements such as plot, character, point of view, setting, symbolism, theme, structure, and interpretation.

●●○ Meaningful alignment

Provides meaningful fiction selections and useful instruction in several elements of fiction, though the range of texts or depth of instruction may need supplementation.

●○○ Limited alignment

Includes some fiction or basic fictional terminology but provides limited range or limited direct instruction in close reading and interpretation of fiction.

○○○ Not a significant focus

Provides little or no meaningful fiction content or instruction.

Calibration example: Strong = multiple stories or longer works paired with instruction in narrative elements and guided analysis; Limited = fiction selections with no supporting instruction.
5 Diverse Literary Voices View rating descriptions
●●● Strong alignment

Provides a substantial range of literary voices across cultures, ethnicities, genders, sexual orientations, socioeconomic experiences, historical contexts, and/or national or global traditions, integrating that diversity meaningfully into literary study.

●●○ Meaningful alignment

Provides a meaningful range of authors and perspectives beyond a narrow canonical sample, though additional voices or traditions may be needed for fuller breadth.

●○○ Limited alignment

Includes some diverse authors or perspectives, but representation is limited, uneven, tokenized, or concentrated in a small portion of the resource.

○○○ Not a significant focus

Provides little meaningful diversity of literary voices, perspectives, cultures, or traditions.

Calibration example: Strong = varied authors and traditions are integrated throughout the resource; Limited = one isolated “diverse voices” unit in an otherwise narrow selection.
6 Literary Terms & Critical Approaches View rating descriptions
●●● Strong alignment

Explicitly and substantially teaches literary terminology, genre conventions, formal elements, literary devices, and interpretive or critical approaches that students can apply to multiple texts.

●●○ Meaningful alignment

Provides useful instruction in literary terminology and/or critical approaches and gives students meaningful opportunities to apply them, though some concepts or approaches may need supplementation.

●○○ Limited alignment

Introduces literary terms, devices, or critical approaches but treatment is brief, primarily definitional, or inconsistently applied to literary analysis.

○○○ Not a significant focus

Provides little or no meaningful instruction in literary terminology, formal elements, devices, or critical approaches.

Calibration example: Strong = terminology and critical approaches are taught and then applied to multiple texts; Limited = a glossary or list with no guided application.
7 Close Reading & Literary Analysis View rating descriptions
●●● Strong alignment

Provides substantial, explicit instruction that moves students from observation and annotation to explication, interpretation, defensible claims, and sustained literary analysis supported by close attention to language, form, and textual evidence.

●●○ Meaningful alignment

Provides useful, explicit instruction in several close-reading and analytical practices and meaningful opportunities to develop interpretations with textual evidence, though some depth may need supplementation.

●○○ Limited alignment

Provides opportunities to discuss or write about literature but limited direct instruction in how to move from textual observation to interpretation and sustained analysis.

○○○ Not a significant focus

Provides little or no meaningful instruction in close reading, interpretation, explication, or evidence-based literary analysis.

Calibration example: Strong = students are guided from annotation and observation to an evidence-based interpretive claim; Limited = discussion questions with no instruction in building an interpretation.
8 Research & Writing About Literature View rating descriptions
●●● Strong alignment

Substantially teaches formal academic writing about literature, including thesis development, use of primary texts, secondary research, source evaluation, integration of quotations, documentation, and drafting or revision within conventions of literary analysis.

●●○ Meaningful alignment

Provides meaningful instruction in literary analysis writing and several research or documentation practices, though some aspects of formal writing or secondary research may need supplementation.

●○○ Limited alignment

Provides some literary writing assignments or brief guidance on thesis, quotation, research, or citation but does not offer enough connected instruction to support formal research-based literary writing independently.

○○○ Not a significant focus

Provides little or no meaningful instruction in formal writing about literature, literary research, use of secondary sources, or documentation.

Calibration example: Strong = the resource sequences thesis development, primary-text analysis, secondary research, and a documented literary essay; Limited = a sample essay or assignment prompt with little instructional scaffolding.

How an AI-assisted reviewer should apply this rubric

Score each criterion independently based on explicit instructional content. For resources longer than a single unit, sample across the whole resource, including opening material, multiple body chapters or units, and later chapters, appendices, or support sections when present.

Support each rating with a brief rationale and specific evidence. When coverage is inconsistent, describe the inconsistency instead of averaging it away. The same passage may support more than one criterion.

Most important: distinguish content presence from instruction. A resource that includes literary texts, citations, discussion questions, or assignments should not be scored as though it teaches literary analysis, research, or writing unless it provides meaningful explanation, modeling, guided practice, or scaffolding.

What this content rubric does not determine

I use this rubric to guide an initial AI-assisted review, then check the evidence and ratings myself before publishing the editorial analysis. It is meant to help faculty decide what to investigate first—not to certify that a resource satisfies ENGL C1002 or a local course outline.

It does not determine official CCN compliance, local curriculum approval, articulation, accessibility compliance, licensing compliance, or suitability for every instructor, assignment, or student population.

Faculty should review any OER they plan to adopt in relation to their own course outline, local requirements, accessibility expectations, students, assignments, and instructional goals.
