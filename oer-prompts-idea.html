Examine with IDEA

Apply the IDEA Framework to serious OER finalists without collapsing inclusion, diversity, equity, and anti-racism findings into the course-content score.

Keep the IDEA lens separate and evidence-based

Use the IDEA Framework after you have narrowed the field to resources you are seriously considering. Course Content Alignment and IDEA findings can inform the same adoption decision, but they answer different questions and should remain visibly separate.

Transparency matters: an AI-assisted IDEA crosswalk on this site or in your own workflow is an independent analysis, not an official ASCCC OERI assessment, certification, badge, or endorsement.

Prompt 1: Apply the IDEA Framework to a Finalist

Use this on a resource that has already earned serious consideration through the content-review process.

IDEA CROSSWALKPrompt 1

Evidence-Based IDEA Crosswalk

Apply the framework as a distinct lens with inspectable evidence and explicit uncertainty.

Open full prompt 
You are helping a college faculty member examine one open educational resource (OER) through the ASCCC OERI Inclusion, Diversity, Equity, and Anti-Racism (IDEA) Framework as a SEPARATE lens from course-content alignment.

I will provide:
1. the OER or a stable link / file,
2. the relevant ASCCC OERI IDEA Framework materials or rubric,
3. optional notes from a separate course-content review.

Your job is to create an evidence-based, independent IDEA crosswalk.

IMPORTANT BOUNDARIES
- Do not merge IDEA findings into a course-content score.
- Do not claim this is an official ASCCC OERI review, badge, certification, or endorsement.
- Use the framework categories and rating language I provide rather than inventing a substitute framework.
- Do not infer an author, editor, or represented person's race, ethnicity, gender, disability, sexuality, religion, or other identity from a name, image, or appearance.
- Distinguish what is directly observable in the resource from interpretation.
- If access is partial, say so and lower confidence accordingly.
- "Not found" is not the same as "not present." Use "insufficient evidence" when appropriate.

FOR EACH IDEA AREA
1. State the framework area.
2. Give the most supportable rating or finding using the framework's own scale.
3. Cite specific, inspectable evidence: chapter, section, page, example, image, activity, author/reading selection, or other location.
4. Explain the reasoning briefly.
5. Note counterevidence, mixed evidence, or important limitations.
6. State confidence: High / Medium / Low, with one sentence explaining why.
7. Identify one concrete opportunity for improvement when useful.

END WITH
- Strongest IDEA findings
- Most important concerns or opportunities
- Areas where evidence is mixed or insufficient
- Items a faculty member should verify directly
- A short reminder that this is an independent crosswalk, not an official ASCCC OERI assessment

Prompt 2: Audit an IDEA Crosswalk

Use this when the first pass seems too confident, too neat, or too dependent on weak evidence.

QUALITY CONTROLPrompt 2

Skeptical IDEA Review

Pressure-test claims, identity inferences, missing evidence, and false certainty.

Open full prompt 
You are auditing an AI-generated IDEA Framework crosswalk for an open educational resource.

I will provide:
1. the OER or accessible evidence,
2. the relevant ASCCC OERI IDEA Framework materials,
3. the AI-generated crosswalk to audit.

Do NOT simply rewrite the first review more confidently. Pressure-test it.

CHECK FOR
- ratings that are not supported by inspectable evidence,
- identity claims inferred from names, photos, or appearance,
- broad claims based on one isolated example,
- missing counterevidence or contradictory material,
- confusion between representation and instructional treatment,
- claims based only on a table of contents or description when fuller evidence is needed,
- "not found" treated as proof of absence,
- framework language that has been altered or collapsed,
- IDEA findings improperly merged with course-content alignment,
- language that could imply an official ASCCC OERI assessment or badge.

FOR EACH QUESTIONABLE FINDING, PROVIDE
- original claim / rating,
- why it may be unsupported, overstated, or incomplete,
- evidence that supports or complicates it,
- recommended action: Keep / Revise / Mark uncertain / Investigate further.

END WITH
- findings that appear well supported,
- findings that most need faculty verification,
- any framework areas where evidence is insufficient,
- whether the crosswalk appears ready for human editorial review.

Prompt 3: Compare IDEA Findings Across Finalists

Use this only after each finalist has its own completed crosswalk. The goal is to surface meaningful differences, not manufacture a single IDEA winner.

SIDE-BY-SIDEPrompt 3

Compare Completed IDEA Crosswalks

Keep framework areas distinct while surfacing tradeoffs, concerns, and supplementation opportunities.

Open full prompt 
You are helping a college faculty member compare IDEA Framework findings across 2–4 OER finalists.

I will provide the completed IDEA crosswalk for each finalist. These crosswalks should already have been reviewed for evidence quality.

Your job is to COMPARE, not rescore.

RULES
- Preserve the framework's distinct areas; do not create one overall IDEA score.
- Do not merge IDEA findings with course-content ratings.
- Compare only claims supported by the crosswalk evidence provided.
- Surface mixed evidence and uncertainty rather than forcing a winner.
- Do not infer identities that are not explicitly and reliably established.

OUTPUT
1. A side-by-side comparison by IDEA area.
2. For each finalist: strongest findings, most important concerns, and areas needing verification.
3. Meaningful differences among the finalists that could matter for teaching.
4. Opportunities for supplementation or adaptation for each finalist.
5. Questions faculty should ask before making an adoption decision.
6. A short synthesis that describes tradeoffs without ranking the resources overall.

IDEA-review guardrails

Do not infer identity.

Names, photos, or appearance are not reliable evidence of race, ethnicity, gender, disability, sexuality, religion, or other identity.

One example is not the whole resource.

Look across the text and note both supporting and complicating evidence.

Partial access means partial confidence.

A preview or table of contents cannot support the same certainty as a full-text review.

Keep Content and IDEA separate.

The two lenses can inform one decision without being collapsed into one score.

Use the actual framework.

Provide the current ASCCC OERI framework materials rather than asking AI to reconstruct the rubric from memory.

Faculty make the final call.

AI can organize evidence; faculty remain responsible for interpretation, context, and adoption decisions.

What comes after the IDEA review?

Carry the completed Content Rubric findings and IDEA crosswalk findings forward as two separate sets of evidence. The final comparison should make their tradeoffs visible without blending them into a hidden total score.

Typical handoff: Content findings + IDEA findings → compare finalists → identify supplementation and tradeoffs → faculty decision.
