PROMPT BANK · COURSE FIT

Match OER to the Course You Already Teach

For faculty who already have a sequence, assignments, priorities, or teaching approach—and want resources that fit the course rather than forcing the course to fit the book.

Your course design is useful search information

A COR can tell us what a course is expected to teach. It cannot tell us how you have organized those expectations into modules, what your major assignments ask students to do, which material you already teach well, or what kind of textbook would actually make your work easier.

Course requirement ≠ course design. These prompts use your existing structure and priorities as fit criteria without pretending that your personal organization is the only correct way to teach the course.

What kind of fit matters most?

I want a resource that fits my whole existing course.Start with Find OER for My Existing Course.
I want chapters that map to my modules.Use Match OER to My Modules.
I want resources that support my assignment sequence.Use Match OER to My Assignments.
I want materials compatible with how I teach.Use Match My Teaching Approach.
I do not want a book that duplicates what I already do.Use Find a Complement, Not a Duplicate.

Five kinds of course fit

🗺️WHOLE COURSESequence + assignments + priorities
🧱MODULESWhere chapters plug in
📝ASSIGNMENTSWhat prepares students to do the work
🧭APPROACHHow the resource teaches
🧩COMPLEMENTWhat the OER can take off your plate
This is still discovery and fit screening. These prompts can help identify promising matches, but a course-specific Content Rubric is the better tool for the deeper question of how much meaningful, teachable course coverage a finalist actually provides.

Prompt 1: Find OER for My Existing Course

This is the broadest prompt in this bank and the best default when you already have a working course.

START HEREPrompt 1

Find OER That Fits My Existing Course

Use your modules, assignments, themes, modality, and existing materials to search for structurally compatible OER.

Open full prompt 
You are helping a college faculty member find openly licensed resources that fit an EXISTING COURSE DESIGN.

I will provide:
1. the course / discipline,
2. my module or unit sequence,
3. my major assignments,
4. optional notes about themes, readings, modality, student population, or teaching approach,
5. optional information about what I already provide myself.

Your job is to search for OER that fit the course I have already designed.

IMPORTANT DISTINCTION

Separate:
- COURSE REQUIREMENTS — what the course must teach, from
- COURSE DESIGN — how I have chosen to organize and teach it.

Do not treat my current design choices as universal course requirements.

STEP 1 — MAP MY COURSE DESIGN

Summarize:
- module / unit sequence,
- major assignments,
- recurring skills,
- thematic or disciplinary emphases,
- resources I already provide,
- places where an OER appears to need to carry more of the instructional load.

STEP 2 — IDENTIFY THE KIND OF RESOURCE I ACTUALLY NEED

Before searching, infer the most plausible role:
- full course spine,
- writing / research handbook,
- reader / anthology,
- disciplinary content text,
- modular companion,
- ancillary / activity source,
- targeted supplement,
- combination of several resources.

If more than one role seems plausible, say so.

STEP 3 — SEARCH FOR FIT

When live web access is available, search:
- established OER repositories,
- Pressbooks and institution-hosted Pressbooks,
- LibreTexts / LibreCommons,
- Open Textbook Library,
- OER Commons,
- MERLOT,
- ASCCC OERI,
- OASIS / Cool4Ed,
- relevant disciplinary or institutional OER collections,
- ordinary web search for openly licensed resources that may not be well indexed.

Search using BOTH:
A. course-content terminology, and
B. language from my actual module sequence, assignments, themes, and approach.

STEP 4 — LOOK FOR STRUCTURAL FIT, NOT JUST TOPIC MATCH

For each candidate, consider:
- whether its organization roughly maps to my sequence,
- whether it supports or conflicts with my assignment flow,
- whether chapters can be assigned modularly,
- whether it duplicates material I already teach well,
- whether it fills places where I currently do heavy supplementation,
- whether it seems usable in my modality,
- whether it appears to offer the right balance of explanation, examples, readings, and practice.

Do not assume a matching table of contents means the resource is a good fit.

STEP 5 — VERIFY BASIC FACTS

For each candidate:
- provide a direct link,
- author/editor,
- platform,
- open license when verified,
- resource type,
- where you found it,
- what still needs verification.

Do not call free-to-read material OER unless an open license can be verified.

STEP 6 — DO NOT DO THE FINAL EVALUATION YET

Do not:
- assign Content Rubric scores,
- apply the IDEA Framework,
- claim that a resource officially satisfies the COR / CCN / C-ID,
- create a single overall quality score,
- declare a winner.

OUTPUT

1. MY COURSE DESIGN AT A GLANCE
Briefly summarize the structure and priorities you inferred.

2. WHAT KIND OF OER WOULD FIT BEST?
Describe the likely resource role(s).

3. STRONG STRUCTURAL MATCHES
Table:
Resource | Type | Platform | License | Why it may fit my sequence | Assignment / module connections | What may not fit | Direct link

4. USEFUL PARTIAL MATCHES
Identify resources that appear strong for part of the design but would require pairing or supplementation.

5. DUPLICATION / REDUNDANCY RISKS
Note resources that may repeat material I already provide rather than solving a real need.

6. POSSIBLE COMBINATIONS
Suggest up to 3 small, manageable combinations if no single resource appears to fit the course well.

7. MOST PROMISING NEXT LOOKS
Recommend 2–5 resources to inspect more closely.

8. WHAT TO CHECK NEXT
List the specific chapters, modules, assignments, or features I should inspect before moving a resource into full Content Rubric review.

Prompt 2: Match OER to My Modules

Useful when you want an OER that can plug into your existing sequence without rebuilding the semester around a textbook's chapter order.

MODULE FITPrompt 2

Map OER to My Module Sequence

Look for modular resources and show exactly where likely chapters or sections could fit.

Open full prompt 
You are helping a college faculty member match OER to an EXISTING MODULE OR UNIT SEQUENCE.

I will provide:
1. the course / discipline,
2. a list of modules or units,
3. the main purpose of each module,
4. optional assignments or readings connected to each one.

Your job is to find OER that could plug into this sequence with minimal restructuring.

STEP 1 — BUILD A MODULE MAP

Create a concise table:
Module | Main concepts / skills | What kind of OER support seems useful

STEP 2 — SEARCH FOR MODULAR RESOURCES

Prioritize resources that:
- have clearly separated chapters or modules,
- allow selective assignment rather than requiring linear use,
- offer sections that map to several of my units,
- can be linked or assigned independently,
- do not require me to rebuild the entire course around the textbook.

Search established OER repositories, institutional OER collections, Pressbooks, LibreTexts, and the broader web when live access is available.

STEP 3 — MAP CANDIDATES TO MY COURSE

For each plausible resource, identify:
- which modules it may support well,
- which modules it only partially supports,
- which modules appear missing,
- whether the chapter order can be rearranged,
- whether the resource seems better as a spine or as a modular companion.

STEP 4 — VERIFY

Provide:
- title,
- author/editor,
- platform,
- direct link,
- license status,
- resource type,
- specific chapters/sections that appear relevant.

If licensing is unclear, write "License needs verification."

Do not assign Content Rubric scores yet.
Do not apply the IDEA Framework.
Do not claim a resource fully fits the course based on titles alone.

OUTPUT

1. Module map
2. Candidate-to-module matrix
3. Best candidates for broad module coverage
4. Best modular companions
5. Modules still needing material
6. Most promising 2–5 resources to inspect next

Prompt 3: Match OER to My Assignment Sequence

Starts with what students actually have to do and searches for explanation, models, examples, and practice that help them get there.

ASSIGNMENT FITPrompt 3

Find OER That Supports My Assignments

Search for resources that prepare students for the moves required by your actual assignments.

Open full prompt 
You are helping a college faculty member find OER that support an EXISTING ASSIGNMENT SEQUENCE.

I will provide:
1. the course / discipline,
2. my major assignments,
3. the skills or knowledge students need before each assignment,
4. optional notes about where students tend to struggle.

Your job is to identify OER that can support the learning path into those assignments.

STEP 1 — ANALYZE THE ASSIGNMENT SEQUENCE

For each assignment, identify:
- what students are being asked to produce or do,
- prerequisite knowledge / reading / reasoning / writing moves,
- what kind of instructional material would help,
- likely places where examples or practice matter.

Do not redesign my assignments unless I ask.

STEP 2 — SEARCH FOR SUPPORTING OER

Search for resources that provide:
- explicit instruction,
- worked examples,
- models,
- guided practice,
- research / source-use support,
- genre instruction,
- revision support,
- ancillaries or activities relevant to the assignments.

STEP 3 — DISTINGUISH EXPLANATION FROM SUPPORT

A chapter that mentions a skill is not necessarily useful preparation for an assignment.

For each candidate, note whether it offers:
- explanation only,
- examples / models,
- practice,
- assignment scaffolding,
- all or several of the above.

STEP 4 — VERIFY OPENNESS AND ACCESS

For each resource provide:
- direct link,
- author / creator,
- platform,
- license status,
- relevant chapter / module / section,
- what assignment(s) it appears to support.

Do not infer open licensing.
If unclear, write "License needs verification."

OUTPUT

1. Assignment-to-skill map
2. Resources that support multiple assignments
3. Resources that strongly support one assignment
4. Gaps in the assignment sequence
5. Possible companion-resource combinations
6. Best resources to inspect next
7. What faculty should verify before adoption

Prompt 4: Match My Teaching Approach

Useful when pedagogy matters as much as coverage—for example, rhetorical, genre-based, inquiry-driven, process-oriented, multimodal, or linguistic-justice-informed instruction.

PEDAGOGICAL FITPrompt 4

Find OER Compatible with How I Teach

Translate your teaching approach into observable resource features and search for evidence of real pedagogical fit.

Open full prompt 
You are helping a college faculty member find OER that fit a PARTICULAR TEACHING APPROACH.

I will provide:
1. the course / discipline,
2. the teaching approach or philosophy I want the course materials to support,
3. optional examples of what that looks like in my class,
4. optional topics, assignments, or materials I already use.

Examples of teaching approaches might include:
- rhetorical rather than formulaic writing instruction,
- writing-about-writing,
- genre-based instruction,
- inquiry-based learning,
- culturally responsive teaching,
- linguistic justice,
- reading-to-writing integration,
- project-based learning,
- process-oriented writing,
- multimodal composition,
- literature organized by themes rather than chronology.

Your job is to find OER whose ORGANIZATION, PEDAGOGY, EXAMPLES, OR ACTIVITIES appear compatible with my approach.

IMPORTANT:
Do not equate a resource with a teaching philosophy merely because it uses similar buzzwords.
Look for inspectable evidence in chapter organization, framing, examples, assignments, activities, or author statements.

STEP 1 — TRANSLATE MY APPROACH INTO SEARCHABLE FEATURES

Identify:
- key terms,
- related pedagogical language,
- observable features an OER might contain,
- features that would conflict with my approach.

STEP 2 — SEARCH

When live web access is available, search established OER sources and the broader web for openly licensed materials.

Do not search only by philosophy label.
Also search by the concrete features you identified.

STEP 3 — CHECK APPARENT PEDAGOGICAL FIT

For each candidate, note:
- evidence that appears compatible,
- places where the fit is uncertain,
- possible conflicts or mismatches,
- whether the resource would require substantial reframing by the instructor.

STEP 4 — VERIFY

Provide direct links and license information when verifiable.
Do not call free material OER without an open license.

Do not assign Content Rubric scores.
Do not apply the IDEA Framework unless requested.
Do not claim a resource embodies a pedagogy unless the evidence supports that conclusion.

OUTPUT

1. Teaching approach translated into observable OER features
2. Search terms used
3. Strong apparent matches
4. Partial / mixed matches
5. Potential mismatches
6. Best resources to inspect next
7. Specific chapters / assignments / features faculty should inspect to verify the fit

Prompt 5: Find a Complement, Not a Duplicate

This is for faculty who already supply a lot of strong instruction themselves and want the OER to fill specific burdens rather than repeat them.

COMPLEMENTARY FITPrompt 5

Find OER That Fills My Course Gaps

Separate what you already provide from what you want a resource to handle, then search for complementarity.

Open full prompt 
You are helping a college faculty member find an OER that COMPLEMENTS what they already teach well.

I will provide:
1. the course / discipline,
2. what I already teach or provide effectively,
3. what I wish an OER would handle for me,
4. optional candidate OER I am already considering.

Your job is to avoid recommending resources that mostly duplicate my strengths.

STEP 1 — SEPARATE "ALREADY COVERED" FROM "NEEDS SUPPORT"

Create two concise lists:
A. Instructor / course already provides
B. OER needs to provide

STEP 2 — SEARCH FOR COMPLEMENTARY RESOURCES

Prioritize OER that are strong in the "needs support" areas.
Do not reward a resource merely because it is comprehensive if most of that comprehensiveness duplicates what I already provide.

STEP 3 — ASSESS LIKELY COMPLEMENTARITY

For each resource, identify:
- what burden it could realistically take off the instructor,
- what it duplicates,
- what it still leaves uncovered,
- whether it seems best as a core text, companion, reader, or targeted supplement.

STEP 4 — VERIFY

Provide:
- title,
- author/editor,
- platform,
- direct link,
- open license when verified,
- relevant chapters / modules / features.

Do not assign final Content Rubric ratings yet.
Do not apply IDEA ratings.
Do not declare a winner.

OUTPUT

1. Already covered vs. needs support
2. Best complementary candidates
3. Duplication risks
4. Candidate role recommendations
5. Possible resource combinations
6. Best 2–5 next looks
7. What to inspect before deeper evaluation

Course-fit guardrails

Fit is not compliance.

A resource may fit your course design beautifully without, by itself, satisfying every course-content expectation.

Do not reward duplication.

If you already teach a part of the course effectively, more textbook coverage there may add little value.

Look beyond the table of contents.

Structural fit depends on how chapters teach, model, and scaffold—not just whether the right nouns appear in chapter titles.

Pedagogy needs evidence.

Do not label a text “rhetorical,” “inclusive,” “genre-based,” or “student-centered” based on marketing language alone.

Modularity matters.

A strong resource can be hard to use if its chapters depend heavily on a fixed sequence that conflicts with your course.

Faculty priorities are contextual.

The resource that fits one instructor's course design may not be the best fit for another instructor teaching the same COR.

What comes next?

Once course-fit searching gives you a manageable set of plausible resources, move from “this seems compatible” to “what does the evidence actually show?”

Typical handoff: course-fit search → shortlist → Content Rubric review → IDEA Framework → compare finalists → faculty decision.
