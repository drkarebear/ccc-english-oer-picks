About the project

A practical faculty guide to a very large OER landscape.

CCC English OER Picks is an independent faculty project created and currently maintained by Karen Crozer. It helps California Community College English faculty compare open resources, understand what each appears to teach well, and decide what may be worth investigating first. The project is maintained on a volunteer basis and is open to faculty feedback, corrections, teaching experience, rubric review, and other contributions.

Why this exists

California Community College faculty already have access to a growing collection of excellent open educational resources. The challenge is no longer simply finding OER. It is deciding which resources are the strongest fit for a particular course, what each resource does especially well, and where additional materials may still be needed.

This site was created and is currently maintained by Karen Crozer, a California Community College English faculty member, as a way to make OER comparison more manageable. The goal is not to establish one person's rankings as definitive. Instead, the project makes its criteria and editorial judgments visible so they can be examined, questioned, corrected, and strengthened over time.

What the project is trying to do

Compare by course

Resources are evaluated differently for C1000, C1001, C1002, and C1003 because the courses ask faculty and students to do different work.

Show strengths and gaps

A useful OER does not need to do everything. The site highlights likely strengths, limitations, and the role a resource might best play.

Separate editorial analysis from faculty experience

Editorial alignment observations describe what a resource appears to teach. Faculty recommendations and teaching notes describe what instructors experienced when using it. The site keeps those forms of evidence separate rather than blending them into one score.

Stay open to correction

Editorial observations are not permanent verdicts. Ratings, evidence, notes, and interpretations may change as resources are revised, deeper review uncovers new evidence, or faculty contribute additional context.

How the initial alignment review works

Initial alignment reviews use course-specific rubrics developed from CCN course expectations and related curriculum guidance. AI assists with reviewing large OER, locating relevant instructional evidence, and applying the rubric consistently. Each editorial review is then examined by the project maintainer before publication.

Ratings are based on explicit instructional coverage, not simply whether a resource contains a topic, reading, assignment, or opportunity for students to practice a skill. Where possible, reviews sample multiple sections of a resource and record the evidence supporting each criterion.

These observations are provisional editorial judgments. They may change when a resource is revised, when stronger evidence becomes available, or when faculty experience complicates the initial review.

These observations are meant to help faculty decide what to investigate, not to certify that a resource satisfies a course outline, local curriculum, articulation requirement, or any other institutional standard.

The alignment ratings are not grades or overall rankings. Each criterion is evaluated independently. A Strong Alignment rating means the resource appears to provide substantial, explicit, teachable instruction in that area. A resource can be excellent while receiving Limited Alignment or Not a Significant Focus in categories outside its purpose. The dots shown on course cards are simply a compact visual representation of these four alignment levels.

●●○ Meaningful Alignment

●○○ Limited Alignment

Why faculty feedback matters

A table of contents can tell us a great deal, but it cannot tell us everything about teaching with a resource. Faculty can identify strengths, omissions, accessibility issues, assignment possibilities, sequencing challenges, student responses, and other realities that may not be visible in an initial review.

That is why faculty recommendations and teaching notes are not treated as decoration. They are an independent source of evidence that can reinforce, complicate, or change the project's initial editorial observations.

Currently maintained by one faculty member, open to many contributors

CCC English OER Picks is currently created and maintained as a volunteer project by Karen Crozer. It is designed, however, to benefit from many kinds of faculty contribution.

You do not need to take on an ongoing role to help. A contribution might be a teaching note from a resource you have used, a correction to a rating or link, evidence the initial review missed, feedback on a rubric, a suggested OER, or help reviewing a future course guide.
