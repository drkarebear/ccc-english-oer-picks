About the project

A practical faculty guide to a very large OER landscape.

CCC English OER Picks is an independent, volunteer-driven project designed to help California Community College English faculty figure out which open resources are worth investigating first.

Why this exists

California Community College faculty already have access to a growing collection of excellent open educational resources. The challenge is no longer simply finding OER. It is deciding which resources are the strongest fit for a particular course, what each resource does especially well, and where additional materials may still be needed.

This site was founded by Karen Crozer as a way to make that decision-making process more manageable for English faculty. The goal was never to establish one person's rankings as definitive. Instead, the project is designed to make initial comparisons visible, invite scrutiny, and become more useful as additional faculty contribute their own experience.

What the project is trying to do

Compare by course

Resources are evaluated differently for C1000, C1001, C1002, and C1003 because the courses ask faculty and students to do different work.

Show strengths and gaps

A useful OER does not need to do everything. The site highlights likely strengths, limitations, and the role a resource might best play.

Separate analysis from experience

Editorial alignment observations and faculty recommendations are shown as different kinds of evidence rather than being blended into one score.

Stay open to correction

Ratings, notes, links, and interpretations can change as resources are revised and as faculty contribute additional context.

How the initial alignment review works

To make initial review manageable across a large number of OER, the project uses an AI-assisted rubric to compare resources with course-specific CCN criteria. A human editor reviews those results, while faculty recommendations and teaching notes provide an independent source of evidence that can confirm, complicate, or correct the initial assessment.

These observations are meant to help faculty decide what to investigate, not to certify that a resource satisfies a course outline, local curriculum, articulation requirement, or any other institutional standard.

Why faculty feedback matters

A table of contents can tell us a great deal, but it cannot tell us everything about teaching with a resource. Faculty can identify strengths, omissions, accessibility issues, assignment possibilities, sequencing challenges, student responses, and other realities that may not be visible in an initial review.

That is why faculty recommendations and teaching notes are not treated as decoration. They are an independent source of evidence that can reinforce, complicate, or change the project's initial editorial observations.

A volunteer faculty project

CCC English OER Picks is maintained as a volunteer project with contributions from California Community College English faculty and OER practitioners. Faculty who want to help review resources, suggest additions, share teaching experience, or participate in the broader English OER conversation are invited to join the English OER CCC Google Group.

Built beside OERI, not instead of it

ASCCC OERI maintains the comprehensive, discipline-curated collection of English OER. This project is intended as a practical comparison and recommendation layer beside that work, not as a replacement for the official collection or for faculty curriculum review.
